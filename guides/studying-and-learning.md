# 📚 Studying and learning

Claude can be a patient tutor that's available at 2 AM and never gets tired of "can you explain that again?". The trick is to use it to **learn**, not just to get answers.

## 1. Understand a hard topic

Tell it your level and ask for an example.

```
Explain supply and demand. I'm a first-year student and I find
textbook explanations confusing. Use a simple real-life example,
then a slightly harder one.
```

Then go deeper with follow-ups:
- `I get the first part, but why does the price go DOWN when supply goes up?`
- `Give me an analogy with something from everyday life.`
- `Draw it as a simple text diagram.`

### The "explain it 3 ways" trick
```
Explain [topic] in three different ways: once with an analogy,
once step by step, and once with a real-world example.
```
Different explanations click for different people.

## 2. Test yourself (the most powerful one)

Reading feels like learning. Being tested **is** learning.

```
I'm studying for an exam on the French Revolution. Quiz me with
10 questions, one at a time. Wait for my answer before the next
question. Tell me if I'm right, and explain if I'm wrong.
At the end, tell me which topics I should review.
```

Variations:
- `Make the questions harder.`
- `Give me multiple choice this time.`
- `Focus only on the topics I got wrong.`

## 3. Turn your notes into study material

Paste your notes or upload a lecture PDF:

```
Here are my lecture notes. Turn them into:
1. A one-page summary
2. 15 flashcards (question on one side, answer on the other)
3. The 5 things most likely to be on the exam
```

## 4. Get feedback on your work (without cheating)

```
Here's my essay. Don't rewrite it. Instead, act as a strict
teacher and give me feedback: what's strong, what's weak, and
the 3 most important things to improve. I'll fix it myself.
```

This is how you actually get better, and it's allowed in most schools. (Always check your school's rules.)

## 5. Solve problems step by step

For math, physics, or anything with steps:

```
Don't give me the answer. Give me a hint for the first step only.
I'll try, then show you my work.
```

If you're totally stuck:
```
Solve this step by step and explain why you did each step.
Then give me a similar problem to try myself.
```

## 6. Learn a language

```
I'm learning English, level about B1. Let's have a conversation
about my weekend. After each of my messages, first correct my
mistakes briefly, then continue the conversation naturally.
```

More ideas:
- `Give me 10 useful phrases for a job interview in English, with examples.`
- `What's the difference between "make" and "do"? Give lots of examples.`
- `Write a short story using these 10 new words, then quiz me on them.`

Use the **phone app with voice** to practice speaking.

## 7. Make a study plan

```
I have an exam in 3 weeks on [subject]. Topics: [list].
I can study about 2 hours a day on weekdays and 4 hours on weekends.
I'm weakest at [topic]. Make me a day-by-day plan, with review days.
```

## ⚠️ Remember

- Check important facts against your textbook. Claude can be wrong, especially on exact dates, formulas, and quotes.
- If your teacher gave you a specific method, tell Claude: `Use the method we learned in class: [describe it].`
- Copying answers teaches you nothing. Quizzes and feedback teach you a lot.
