# 5. Limits and safety

Claude is very useful, but it's a tool and has weak spots. Knowing them makes you a smarter user.

## 1. It can be confidently wrong

AI assistants sometimes make things up: a fake statistic, a book that doesn't exist, a wrong date, a law that's slightly off. This is often called **"hallucination"**. It doesn't sound unsure when it happens. It sounds exactly as confident as when it's right.

**Always double-check:**
- Numbers, statistics, and dates
- Names, quotes, and sources ("according to a 2019 study...")
- Anything you'll send to a boss, teacher, or client
- Anything medical, legal, or financial

**Helpful habits:**
- Ask: `How confident are you in this? What parts might be wrong?`
- Ask: `What should I double-check before I use this?`
- If it gives a source, look up that source yourself

## 2. It may not know recent things

Claude learned from information up to a certain date. It may not know about recent events, new prices, or this week's news. Some versions can search the web, and when they do, you'll usually see the sources. If you're not sure, ask: `Did you search the web for this, or is it from your training?`

## 3. It's not a professional

It can help you **understand** your blood test, contract, or tax form, and that's genuinely useful. But for real decisions:

- 🏥 Health: talk to a doctor
- ⚖️ Legal: talk to a lawyer
- 💰 Money: talk to a qualified advisor
- 🧠 Mental health crisis: contact a real person or a local helpline

A good use: `I have a doctor's appointment about X. What questions should I ask?`

## 4. Be careful what you share

Treat what you type like something that **could** be seen by others, even though it usually isn't.

**Don't paste:**
- Passwords, PINs, or bank card numbers
- Your passport or ID numbers
- Other people's private information without their permission
- Confidential company documents (check your workplace rules first)

**Fine to share:**
- General details about your situation
- Your own writing, CV, or homework
- Public documents and articles

You can remove names before pasting: replace them with `[Name]` or `Person A`. Claude works just as well.

Check **Settings → Privacy** on [claude.ai](https://claude.ai) to see and control how your data is used.

## 5. School and work rules

Many schools and employers have rules about AI. Using Claude to **learn**, **get feedback**, or **brainstorm** is usually fine. Submitting its writing as your own work often is not. Check the rules where you are, and when in doubt, ask.

A good rule: **use Claude to get better at the thing, not to skip learning the thing.**

## 6. It's agreeable by default

Claude tends to be polite and supportive. That's nice, but sometimes you need honesty. Ask for it:

```
Be brutally honest. What's wrong with this plan? Don't be nice about it.
```
